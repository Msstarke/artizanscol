Guide — For artists

Verification guide for artists

When you submit your profile for review, our team looks at whether the portfolio represents genuine human-made creative output. The strongest submissions include process shots, sketches, or work-in-progress files alongside finished pieces. If your practice involves digital tools, showing layered files or time-lapses helps establish authorship clearly. Profiles that pass review quickly tend to have consistent visual style across the portfolio and a bio that explains the practice in plain terms.

Guide — For clients

How to write a brief that gets results

Artists respond better to booking requests that are specific about scope, timeline, and budget — even rough estimates help. A clear project type (logo, illustration, score, article), a realistic deadline, and an honest budget range gives the artist enough to say yes or no without needing a back-and-forth. Include reference works if you have them. The more concrete the brief, the less revision the project will need later.

Platform update

How beginners get discovered

Discovery on Artizans Collective is not sorted by follower count, booking history, or time on the platform. Clients browse by category, medium, availability, and budget. A complete profile with strong work samples competes on the same terms as a well-established name. If you are early in your career, the most effective thing you can do is keep your profile tight — a handful of your best pieces is more compelling than a large mixed-quality portfolio.
